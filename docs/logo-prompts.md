# dayro. — logo prompts

dayro. is a calm, visual day planner. You drop time blocks onto an hour-by-hour timeline, tick off daily routines and write notes for the day, on the web and on your phone.

---

## 1. The reference logos, up close

### perceptic: a "p" made from two overlapping shapes
- A **D-shaped bowl** (flat left side, full round right side) laid over a **pointer / paper-plane arrow** that points down-left.
- The arrow's tail sticks out of the bowl on the left and at the bottom, which turns the D into a lowercase **p**.
- **Where the two shapes overlap, the color flips** to the background. That creates a small checkerboard in the middle. This one trick is the whole idea.
- Every corner is softly rounded. Solid fill, very heavy.
- Two meanings in one shape: the letter p, plus a cursor/direction (perception).

### Binary: a "B" made from stacked slabs
- A capital **B** built from **horizontal slabs stacked like layers**: top slab, middle bar, bottom slab, with the left stem broken into offset pieces.
- The holes in the B are **horizontal black bars as thick as the white bars**, so it reads as an even stripe rhythm (like 1s and 0s).
- Corners are **cut at 45°**, with a V-notch on the right where the two bowls meet. The slight offsets look like folded paper, with no shading at all.
- Sharp corners only. The wordmark has one custom touch: a dotless "ı".

### Sleuth: an "S" drawn with one line
- An **outline**, not a filled shape: **one line of constant thickness** that loops into **two rounded pill shapes tilted at 45°**, interlocking like chain links or a paperclip.
- One loose straight bar sits inside, completing the S.
- The line ends are cut flat at 45°. The line is about as thick as the letters in the wordmark, so symbol and text feel like one family.
- Two meanings: the letter S, plus links/connections (clues).

### Collective Capital: a cube with a missing piece
- An **isometric cube** seen from above, split into **separate flat planes**: half of the top face (a triangle), the full right face, and a thin strip at the bottom left.
- The **missing piece leaves a dark "step" inside the cube**, like a block being placed into a box.
- No outlines and no shading. The gaps between planes alone create the depth.
- Sharp corners, strict isometric angles.

### Investure: a radial burst with a hidden triangle
- **Eight thick rectangular spokes** with square ends, arranged around a center.
- The spokes are grouped in pairs so that the **empty space in the middle forms a triangle**.
- The bottom spokes form an **upward arrow** (growth).
- Sharp corners, every bar the same width.

### What they all have in common
1. **One idea, at most two meanings** (a letter plus an object: p + pointer, S + link, cube + building block, star + arrow).
2. **Built from 2–5 basic shapes** (rectangles, circles, triangles, pills). Nothing drawn freehand.
3. **Negative space does the work**: overlaps that flip color, gaps between slabs, missing pieces, hidden shapes in the middle.
4. **One strict rule per symbol**: either every corner is rounded or every corner is sharp. Every bar is the same thickness.
5. **Heavy and compact.** Each symbol fits in a square and still works as a tiny app icon.
6. **The symbol carries all the personality.** The wordmark is a plain, neutral sans-serif in regular weight, about as tall as the symbol, set to its right.
7. **No literal pictures**: no lightbulbs, magnifying glasses, coins or calendars with rings.

## 2. Why the results keep repeating, and why variety matters

**Why it happens:**
- The old prompt named **one concrete shape** ("a k built from rounded rectangles"), so every run lands on that same idea and only the details change.
- The same prompt with similar settings gives the generator the same starting point every time.
- Chat-based generators (ChatGPT, Gemini) **reuse the previous image** as a reference for the next one in the same chat.

**Why variety matters:** at this stage you're choosing an **idea**, not polishing one. Ten versions of the same "k from blocks" only tell you how that one idea can look. Ten truly different ideas show you which direction actually fits dayro. Real logo designers sketch dozens of different directions first and narrow down later. Do the same here: **go wide first, then refine.**

Real variety means changing **what the symbol shows** (concept) and **how it's built** (technique), not just thickness or corner radius.

## 3. Prompt A: exploration sheet (start here)

One image, nine different ideas. The prompt tells the generator directly that variety is the main goal.

```text
Logo exploration sheet for "dayro.", a calm, visual day-planner app where you place time blocks on an hour-by-hour timeline, tick off daily routines and write notes for the day. Show 9 different logo symbols in a 3x3 grid on a pure white background, each in solid flat black, each with the lowercase wordmark "dayro." in a plain neutral grotesk sans-serif, regular weight, next to it.

VARIETY IS THE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT. Every one of the 9 symbols must be a clearly different idea built with a different technique. No two symbols may share the same base shape, construction or silhouette. Do not make variations of one idea. Mix letter-based marks (a lowercase "k") with abstract marks. Spread these techniques across the grid: two shapes overlapping where the overlap flips to white; horizontal slabs stacked with even gaps and 45-degree cut ends; one continuous line of constant thickness folded into a shape; flat isometric planes with one piece missing; radial spokes around a hidden negative-space shape; a solid shape with a letter cut out of it.

Style for all: modern, minimal, geometric, heavy and compact, built from only 2 to 5 simple shapes, every bar the same thickness, negative space doing the work, readable at 16 px. Flat vector, black on white only. No color, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D rendering, no mockups. Avoid clichés: no calendar pages with rings, no checkmark in a box, no clock with hands, no letter inside a circle.
```

## 4. Prompt B: one logo at a time, with swappable parts

Use this to explore one direction properly. **Change both the concept and the technique every run.**

```text
Minimal modern logo for "dayro.", a calm, visual day-planner app. Horizontal lockup: a symbol on the left, about as tall as the letters, then the lowercase wordmark "dayro." in a plain neutral grotesk sans-serif, regular weight.

Symbol: [CONCEPT]
Corners: [all softly rounded / all sharp with 45-degree cuts].

The symbol is built from only 2 to 5 simple geometric shapes, heavy and compact, every bar the same thickness, with negative space doing the work. It must still read at 16 px. Solid flat black on pure white. No color, no gradients, no shadows, no 3D rendering, no mockup, no extra text. Avoid calendar pages with rings, checkmarks in boxes and clocks with hands.
```

## 5. Tips for more variety

- **Start with Prompt A**, pick 2–3 directions you like, then refine them with Prompt B.
- **ChatGPT / Gemini:** start a **new chat for each concept**, or they copy the last image. If they stick to one idea, say: "Completely different idea. Don't use rounded rectangles or stacked bars."
- **Midjourney:** run each concept as its own prompt and add `--chaos 50 --style raw --no color, gradient, shadow, mockup`. Raise `--chaos` for wilder results.
- **Ideogram:** turn **Magic Prompt off**, so it doesn't rewrite every prompt into the same default style.
- **Spelling:** if "dayro." comes out wrong, add "symbol only, no text" and set the wordmark yourself.
